1. Introduction

Henna is a flowering shrub best known for the natural reddish-orange dye obtained from its leaves. The plant has been used for centuries for traditional body decoration, hair colouring and other cultural purposes.

Henna grows well in warm and relatively dry regions. It is native to parts of tropical Africa, the Arabian Peninsula, Pakistan and India.

3. Habit & general appearance

Henna is a branched, bushy shrub and may sometimes grow into a small tree. It can reach around 2 m or more depending on growing conditions.

  • 🌿 Bushy and highly branched growth
  • 🍃 Small, opposite leaves
  • 🌸 Numerous small, fragrant flowers
  • 🟤 Small fruits containing many seeds
  • 🌱 Often grows well in warm and relatively dry conditions
4. Leaf

The leaves are one of the most important parts of the henna plant.

  • Leaves are simple.
  • They occur in opposite pairs.
  • Leaf blade is generally elliptical to ovate/obovate.
  • Usually smooth and green.
  • Contain the natural colouring compound responsible for henna's famous stain.

Why important? Leaves are collected, dried and powdered to prepare traditional henna preparations.

5. Stem
  • 🌿 Highly branched
  • 🌳 Woody in mature plants
  • 🟤 Brownish to greyish in older portions
  • 🌱 Capable of producing many small branches

The numerous branches give the plant its characteristic bushy appearance.

6. Flower

Henna produces many small flowers arranged in branched flower clusters called panicles.

  • Small flowers
  • Usually white, cream or yellowish
  • Fragrant
  • Produced in clusters
  • Attractive to insects

The flowers are important in the plant's reproductive process.

7. Fruit and seeds

After flowering, henna develops small fruits.

Fruit: Small and roughly spherical, develops from the flower after fertilization, contains numerous seeds.

Seeds: Small and numerous, help in producing new plants.

8. Reproduction

Henna reproduces mainly through seeds.

🌸 Flower 🐝 Pollination ✨ Fertilization 🟤 Fruit formation 🌱 Seed formation 🌿 New plant

Insects can visit the flowers and contribute to pollination.

9. The secret behind the henna colour

The most famous chemical constituent of henna is lawsone.

🧪 Lawsone – natural orange-red pigment found mainly in henna leaves.

When henna preparation is applied to keratin-containing materials such as hair or nails, lawsone can bind with keratin and produce the characteristic orange-to-reddish-brown stain.

This is why henna is famous as a natural colouring material.

10. Uses of henna

Traditional & cosmetic

  • Body decoration and mehndi
  • Hair colouring
  • Nail staining
  • Cultural and ceremonial purposes

Traditional medicinal – used in various cultures, but traditional use does not automatically mean medically proven.

Other uses – environmental, social and other traditional uses.

11. Ecological importance
  • ☀️ Adapted to warm climates, tolerates dry conditions
  • 🐝 Flowers provide resources for insects
  • 🌿 Performs photosynthesis, releases oxygen
  • 🏡 Provides plant biomass and habitat
12. Growing conditions

Temperature: Warm and hot climates.

Sunlight: Good sunlight beneficial.

Soil: Tolerates relatively dry conditions, range of soils.

Water: Relatively tolerant of dry conditions once established; adequate water for healthy growth.

13. Safety: natural henna vs. “black henna”
Important: Natural henna normally produces an orange, reddish-orange or brownish-red stain.

Products sold as "black henna" may contain additional chemicals, particularly p-phenylenediamine (PPD). PPD can cause serious skin reactions in some people.

⚠️ Natural henna and "black henna" are not necessarily the same thing.

Do not assume that every product labelled "henna" contains only natural henna.

14. Cultural importance

Henna has an important place in traditions across South Asia, the Middle East and parts of Africa.

  • Weddings
  • Festivals
  • Religious and cultural celebrations
  • Traditional body decoration
  • Hair decoration

The practice of applying henna is commonly known as mehndi in South Asia.

15. Interesting facts
  • The scientific name of henna is Lawsonia inermis.
  • Belongs to Lythraceae — same family as Lagerstroemia.
  • Characteristic colour comes mainly from lawsone.
  • Leaves are the main plant part used for dye.
  • Natural henna usually produces orange to reddish-brown, not black.
  • Adapted to warm climates; tolerates dry conditions.
